The house

Fifty-eight pieces a day, and not one more.

Marengo began in 1974 in a converted grain store on the hill at Colle Marengo, forty minutes above Perugia. Two rooms, eleven people, a single linking machine. The building is larger now and the machines are quieter, but the daily count has barely moved.

We buy fibre once a year from the same four herds, and we let the cloth rest between stages — a habit inherited from the founder's mother, who held that knitwear, like bread, is ruined by hurry.

01

Fibre

Long-staple and undyed at intake, sorted by hand into eleven natural tones before a single thread is spun.

02

Hand

Every seam is linked by a person. Every finished piece is worn for an hour by the person who made it, then folded.

03

Time

Six weeks from spin to shelf. We have twice been asked to halve it, and have twice declined.
